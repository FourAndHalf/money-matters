## 1. Active vs passive in India (SPIVA, direct vs regular, passive landscape)

### Takeaway
Most active Indian large-cap and ELSS funds trail their S&P benchmarks over 3, 5 and 10 years (about 75-85% of large-cap funds). Mid/small-cap active funds are the one category with periods of majority outperformance, for example calendar 2025. Direct plans cost about 0.65-1.0 percentage points a year less than regular plans in active equity. Passive funds are about 17-18% of Indian MF AUM. An engine's highest-confidence product rules are "prefer direct plans" and "default core equity to low-cost index funds". Suggestions to switch between active funds based on past performance have weak support.

### Cited Findings
- **SPIVA India Year-End 2025 (data to 31 Dec 2025):** active Indian large-cap underperformance against the S&P BSE 100 was 75.0% over 1 year, 74.2% over 3 years, 84.4% over 5 years and 76.3% over 10 years. The S&P India LargeMidCap gained 8.9% in 2025. Large-cap funds returned 7.3% equal-weighted and 9.4% asset-weighted. — [SPIVA India Year-End 2025 (S&P DJI)](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-india-scorecard-year-end-2025.pdf); [S&P DJI SPIVA India page](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india)
- **SPIVA YE2025, ELSS:** 69.2% of ELSS funds underperformed the S&P India BMI in 2025, when the index gained 6.3%. Long-horizon ELSS figures for YE2025 were not retrieved. — [SPIVA India YE2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-india-scorecard-year-end-2025.pdf)
- **SPIVA YE2025, mid/small-cap:** active mid/small-cap funds "delivered a majority outperformance" in 2025, their best relative result since 2014. Yet "a firm majority of funds in every category underperformed over the decade ending in December 2025". — [S&P DJI SPIVA India](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india)
- **SPIVA India Mid-Year 2025 (to 30 Jun 2025):** large-cap underperformance was 65.6% over 6 months and 41.9% over 1 year. 76.9% of ELSS funds trailed the S&P India BMI in H1 2025. — [SPIVA India Mid-Year 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-india-scorecard-mid-year-2025.pdf)
- **10-year figures to June 2025 (secondary, unverified):** 73% of large-cap, 82% of mid/small-cap and 87% of ELSS funds underperformed. This is an AI-generated X post citing SPIVA MY2025, so low reliability. — [X/Grok post](https://x.com/grok/status/1986084920276705355). Another site gives an 82.4% 10-year large-cap figure, which conflicts with SPIVA's official 76.3% for YE2025. — [FundsAge](https://www.fundsageai.com/blog/can-fund-managers-beat-the-market-spiva-india)
- **Survivorship:** about 27% of funds across the five Indian categories did not survive the 10 years to Dec 2025. This comes from secondary reporting and was not confirmed in the PDF. — [S&P DJI SPIVA India](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india)
- **Benchmark critique:** one Indian adviser argues that the post-2024 SPIVA benchmark choice (S&P BSE 100 / LargeMidCap) overstates large-cap underperformance against funds' actual investible universe. — [BasuNivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/active-vs-passive-why-you-must-not-trust-spiva-india-report/)
- **Direct vs regular TER gap:** regular-plan equity TER averages about 1.03 pp above direct, or about 90 bp AUM-weighted. That analysis is about 2023 vintage. — [PrimeInvestor](https://primeinvestor.in/reports/mf-direct-vs-regular-plans-an-expense-ratio-guide/). One scheme example from June 2025: direct 1.46% vs regular 2.49%. — [Dhan](https://dhan.co/mutual-funds/direct-vs-regular/). A blog citing a 500-scheme dataset gives an average gap of 0.65 pp for active equity and 0.35 pp for debt (dataset unverified). — [Rupaywise](https://rupaywise.com/guides/direct-vs-regular-mutual-funds)
- **Academic:** over 2013-2023, direct plans beat regular plans by about 1.49%/yr in small-cap and about 1%/yr in large-cap, consistent with the expense gap. — [ResearchGate 2025 paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392652036_Risk-Adjusted_Performance_Comparison_of_Direct_and_Regular_Plan_Equity_Mutual_Funds_in_India)
- **SEBI TER reform (unverified):** a blog says SEBI reforms of Dec 2025, effective April 2026, replace TER with a new cost measure. This was not confirmed against a primary SEBI source. — [Genvest](https://www.genvest.ai/blog/direct-vs-regular-mutual-funds)
- **Passive AUM:** ₹14.11-14.12 lakh crore on 31 Mar 2026, up about 23% YoY, with about 5.71 crore folios. — [Finnovate (AMFI data)](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/passive-funds-fy26-india-aum-folio-growth); [AMFI Monthly Note Mar 2026](https://www.amfiindia.com/uploads/AMFI_Monthly_Note_Mar_2026_1f0a1f5c48.pdf). Passive funds held 17.4% of industry AUM in Dec 2025 — [Business Standard topic page](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/topic/passive-funds) — and nearly 18% in Sep 2026 (index funds plus ETFs ₹14.70 lakh crore) — [Tata MF](https://www.tatamutualfund.com/blogs/mutual-fund-aum-india-category-wise-breakdown-and-ten-year-trend). Gold and silver ETFs are a fast-growing slice. Index funds took ₹8,169 cr of inflows in Mar 2026. — [AMFI Mar 2026 note](https://www.amfiindia.com/uploads/AMFI_Monthly_Note_Mar_2026_1f0a1f5c48.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Switch regular to direct" is the most certain and largest recurring saving an engine can surface: about 0.65-1.0 pp/yr on active equity. It compounds to a large share of terminal wealth over 20 years. The engine should show the TER delta in rupees per year, computed from holdings. The exit load and capital-gains tax cost of switching must be netted off, because a switch is a redemption.
- "Large-cap active fund → Nifty 50/100 index fund" is strongly supported. "Mid/small-cap active → index" is less clear-cut, because SPIVA shows category cyclicality, for example majority outperformance in 2025.
- Fund-picking by trailing returns is undermined by SPIVA persistence data. Persistence tables were not retrieved here; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- The exact YE2025 3/5/10-year underperformance for ELSS and mid/small-cap, the survivorship table, and SPIVA India persistence scorecards. The S&P PDF could not be fetched in this session.
- AMFI category-wise TER averages for 2026 (direct vs regular) from a primary source.
- Whether SEBI's 2025-26 TER restructuring (base expense ratio / exclusion of statutory levies) is in force. Unconfirmed.

## 2. Factor / smart-beta evidence in Indian equities

### Takeaway
Indian factor indices (momentum, alpha, quality, value, low-vol) have long-run backtests ahead of the Nifty 200/500. The cost of that edge is deep drawdowns: momentum and alpha fell about 68-79% peak-to-trough historically. Factor leadership is strongly cyclical. Momentum had a negative 1-year stretch in 2025-26, while value led over the 5 years to Aug 2026. Most index histories are back-tested before launch dates. An engine should therefore treat factor tilts as an optional satellite with explicit drawdown warnings, not as a reliable "opportunity".

### Cited Findings
- **NSE momentum whitepaper (April 2026):** Nifty200 Momentum 30 maximum drawdown is reported as 68%, versus a smaller drawdown for the parent Nifty 200. Snippets show momentum-family drawdowns of 68-73%. — [NSE Indices, "The Science of Momentum", Apr 2026](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/momentum-strategy-whitepaper_2026.pdf)

A Mirae document cites max drawdown −34.0% with a 13.9-month recovery for a momentum + G-Sec 50:50 product. That is a blended product, not pure momentum. — [Mirae Asset presentation](https://www.miraeassetmf.co.in/docs/default-source/product-presentations/mirae-asset-nifty200-momentum-30-plus-8-13-yr-g-sec-5050-etf-presentation.pdf)

- **Global decay of published anomalies:** across 97 predictors, returns were 26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower after publication (McLean and Pontiff, J. Finance 2016). Decay was larger for predictors with higher in-sample t-stats. — [McLean & Pontiff (Ivey PDF)](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf); [Gwern mirror](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)

### Inferences
- Pre-launch index history is a backtest: Alpha 50's history before 2012 and Quality 30's before 2018. Combined with McLean-Pontiff decay of about 58% post-publication, forward factor premia in India should be assumed well below backtested CAGRs.
- Momentum's semi-annual reconstitution with about 19-20 of 30 names changing implies high turnover. Replicating it at home would incur STCG at 20% and STT on most gains. It is far more tax-efficient inside an index fund/ETF, where turnover is not taxed at the investor level.
- A defensible engine rule: if a user holds factor funds, report factor exposure and drawdown history. Do not generate "rotate into the factor that did best recently" signals; 2021-26 leadership shows reversals.

### Gaps

- Calendar-year 2024 and 2025 returns for each factor index from a primary source.
- Recent IIMA factor premia (e.g. momentum premium 2015-2025). The data files could not be fetched.

## 3. Portfolio-level "opportunities" that reliably add value

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence is for mechanical, cost- and tax-driven actions:
- direct-plan switching
- lump-sum investing when cash is already available (beats spreading about two-thirds of the time)
- annual or semi-annual rebalancing with about 5-percentage-point bands
- using the ₹1.25 lakh/yr equity LTCG exemption (tax-gain harvesting)
- parking surplus in arbitrage funds rather than liquid funds for high-slab taxpayers with horizons over 12 months

These are "opportunities" with near-certain or high-probability payoffs, unlike return forecasting.

### Cited Findings
- **Indian capital gains rules (Budget July 2024, unchanged in Budget 2026):** equity LTCG is 12.5% above a ₹1.25 lakh/yr exemption (Sec 112A). Equity STCG (held 12 months or less) is 20%. — [Business Standard, Jul 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/budget-2024-hikes-ltcg-tax-rate-to-12-5-stcg-to-20-stt-on-f-o-also-up-124072300553_1.html); [Angel One, Budget 2026](https://www.angelone.in/news/taxation/union-budget-2026-ltcg-tax-unchanged-at-12-5-stt-on-commodity-futures-hiked); [Tata MF post-Budget 2026](https://www.tatamutualfund.com/blogs/post-budget-2026-new-tax-map-equity-investors)
- **Shared exemption:** the ₹1.25 lakh limit is shared across listed equity, equity-oriented funds and business-trust units. — [Bajaj AMC](https://www.bajajamc.com/knowledge-centre/arbitrage-fund-taxation)
- **Debt and liquid funds:** for units bought on or after 1 Apr 2023, gains are taxed at slab rate regardless of holding period. — [Bajaj AMC](https://www.bajajamc.com/knowledge-centre/arbitrage-fund-taxation); [Fincart](https://www.fincart.com/blog/arbitrage-fund-taxation)
- **Arbitrage funds:** taxed as equity if at least 65% is in listed Indian shares. Example for a 30%-slab investor at a 7% gross return: liquid fund nets about 4.9%, an arbitrage fund held over 12 months nets about 6.1% (illustrative; ignores fees and cess). — [Bajaj AMC](https://www.bajajamc.com/knowledge-centre/arbitrage-fund-taxation); [Fincart](https://www.fincart.com/blog/arbitrage-fund-taxation). One older source still cites 10% LTCG, which is outdated. — [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/stock-market-guide/mutual-funds/arbitrage-vs-liquid-funds)
- **Lump sum vs DCA:** Vanguard research found lump sum beat 12-month DCA about two-thirds of the time (about 66-68%, regional range about 62-74%). The size of the edge is reported variously, with secondary sources not agreeing. Lump sum has worse tail outcomes, and DCA's value is mainly behavioural. — [Greenline summary](https://usegreenline.com/articles/dollar-cost-averaging-vs-lump-sum); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/analysis/us-dollarcost-averaging-vs-lumpsum-investing-why-safer-strategy-underperforms-200673577); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/invest-once-spread-heres-vanguard-205817086.html) (all secondary)
- **Rebalancing frequency (Vanguard; Jaconetti, Kinniry, Zilbering):** no material difference in risk-adjusted outcomes from monthly to annual rebalancing. More frequent rebalancing mainly raises turnover. — [Vanguard research summary](https://corpaemdisp.essp.c1.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/articles/tuning-frequency-for-rebalancing.html); [Kitces](https://www.kitces.com/blog/best-opportunistic-rebalancing-frequency-time-horizons-vs-tolerance-band-thresholds/)
- **Rebalancing bands:** quoted guidance is "for most broadly diversified stock and bond portfolios, annual or semi-annual monitoring, with rebalancing at 5 percent thresholds, produced a reasonable balance between risk control and cost minimization". This is a secondary quote of Vanguard. — [WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/investment/art-rebalancing). Smaller thresholds mean lower tracking error but higher cost. Threshold-only rebalancing needs daily monitoring, so calendar-plus-threshold is the practical hybrid. — [Vanguard research summary](https://corpaemdisp.essp.c1.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/articles/tuning-frequency-for-rebalancing.html); [Vanguard "Rational rebalancing"](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/rational_rebalancing_analytical_approach_to_multiasset_portfolio_rebalancing.pdf)
- **SIPs vs investor behaviour in India (Axis MF study, 2003-2020):** equity funds returned 18.7%/yr and equity SIPs 14.5%, but the average equity investor got 13.2%. A later Axis study (2003-2022) reports 19.1% fund CAGR vs 13.8% investor returns. — [Business Standard, Apr 2021](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pf/returns-made-by-average-mf-investor-lag-those-of-schemes-and-sips-study-121040101236_1.html); [Business Standard/ANI press release, Aug 2023](https://www.business-standard.com/content/press-releases-ani/decoding-wealth-creation-expert-analysis-on-the-behaviour-gap-in-investment-returns-vs-investor-returns-by-santosh-kedari-ceo-sanriya-finvest-123082900013_1.html)

### Inferences
These are reasoned from the rules above; there is no study behind them.
- **Tax-gain harvesting:** each FY, redeem and reinvest equity units held for more than 12 months to realise up to ₹1.25 lakh of LTCG tax-free. This resets cost basis and saves up to ₹15,625/yr (12.5% × 1.25 lakh) of future tax per person. It is a certain-payoff opportunity. Costs: exit loads (usually none after 12 months), a short out-of-market gap, and resetting the holding clock, which matters only if new units are then sold within 12 months. Other constraints:
  - Do it before 31 March.
  - The limit is per PAN and shared across equity and equity-fund gains.
  - Grandfathering of 31 Jan 2018 cost basis complicates old holdings.
- **Tax-loss harvesting:** realising losses to offset gains is a legitimate opportunity. Engine logic must follow Indian set-off rules (STCL against STCG or LTCG; LTCL only against LTCG; 8-year carry-forward with timely ITR filing). These rules were not sourced here; verify them. There is no wash-sale rule in Indian law, but tax authorities can challenge sham transactions. Verify before shipping.
- **Cash-drag sweep:** for a 30% slab user with a 12+ month horizon, arbitrage funds beat liquid funds after tax by about 1.2 pp in the cited example. For money needed within months, liquid funds or FDs are better because arbitrage STCG is 20% and there are exit loads.
- **Rebalancing:** the engine should trigger a rebalance only when allocation drifts by 5 pp or more at a semi-annual or annual check. It should prefer rebalancing with new inflows (SIP redirection) over selling, to avoid STCG/LTCG.
- **Consolidating overlapping funds:** reduces cost and complexity, but every exit is a taxable redemption. The engine should net out tax before recommending.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for FD laddering and rate-shopping. The practical evidence is the spread between bank FD rates (small finance banks vs PSU banks), which needs live rate data. DICGC insurance is ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank; this is not sourced here, so verify it.
- No Indian study found on emergency-fund sizing. Common practice is 3-6 months of expenses, but no rigorous source was retrieved.
- No Indian-market lump-sum vs SIP study retrieved. The Vanguard figures are US/UK/Australia, and the primary Vanguard paper was not fetched.
- No quantitative study on the benefit of fund-overlap consolidation.

## 4. Robo-advisor / goal-based methodology and Indian robo platforms

### Takeaway
Indian "robo" platforms are mostly goal-based, direct-plan MF distribution and planning tools. SEBI rules require explicit client approval, which limits full automation. No primary methodology documents (glide paths, optimisation method) were found. A personal engine should rely on transparent simple rules (target allocation by goal horizon, band rebalancing, direct index funds) rather than mean-variance optimisation.

### Cited Findings
- Robo advice in India falls under SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations 2013. Platforms cannot execute buy/sell orders without explicit client instruction, so they are semi-automated. — [Hisabhkaro](https://hisabhkaro.com/learn/what-is-robo-advisor-india/); [Jainam](https://www.jainam.in/blog/role-of-robo-advisors/)
- Kuvera's parent is a SEBI RIA (INA200005166, registered 19 Jul 2016) and offers free goal-based planning with direct plans. — [Wikipedia: Kuvera.in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuvera.in)
- One market roundup lists Scripbox for goals, INDmoney for multi-asset/hybrid AI plus human advice, and Groww for beginners with simple direct MF. — [Inventiva](https://www.inventiva.co.in/trends/robo-advisors-india-2025/); [Hisabhkaro](https://hisabhkaro.com/learn/ai-investing-robo-advisors-india/)
- Kuvera's and INDmoney's goal tools reportedly project goals with inflation and alert users who fall behind trajectory. — [Hisabhkaro](https://hisabhkaro.com/learn/ai-investing-robo-advisors-india/) (secondary, vendor-adjacent)
- Morningstar found allocation funds (largely target-date funds with glide paths) had the smallest investor return gap, 0.1 pp over 10 years to 2024. This is indirect evidence that automated rebalancing and glide-path wrappers improve realised returns. — [Morningstar Mind the Gap 2025](https://www.morningstar.com/content/cs-assets/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/blt2c5c4d9171638c42/689b424311f3880edc4b4813/US_Mind_the_Gap_2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The behavioural benefit (smaller return gap) of automated allocation/rebalancing products is better evidenced than any optimiser's return benefit. An offline personal app is best positioned to be a "rule enforcer": goal → horizon-based equity/debt split → band alerts → glide-down as the goal nears.
- Mean-variance optimisation on personal holdings would be fragile because expected-return estimates are noisy (inferred from the factor-zoo and decay evidence in sections 2 and 6). Simple rules or equal-risk heuristics are more robust for a personal engine.

### Gaps
- No primary documentation on the glide paths or asset-allocation algorithms of Kuvera, Scripbox, INDmoney or Groww.
- No sourced comparison of mean-variance vs risk parity vs simple rules for Indian retail.
- No data on Indian robo-platform AUM or user outcomes.

## 5. Signals with weak or negative evidence for retail (TA, news trading, F&O, intraday)

### Takeaway
SEBI's own data shows Indian retail derivative and intraday trading destroys wealth at scale:
- 89% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY22
- 93% across FY22-24, with ₹1.8 lakh crore aggregate losses
- about 91% in FY25, with ₹1.06-1.12 lakh crore losses
- 87.7% in FY26, with about ₹91,685 crore losses
- about 71% of intraday cash traders lost money in FY23

An engine should never produce F&O, intraday or short-term trading signals. Barber-Odean evidence shows that higher trading frequency lowers net returns.

### Cited Findings
- **SEBI Jan 2023 study (FY22):** 89% of individual equity F&O traders lost money. Average loss was about ₹1.1 lakh. The sample was individual clients of the top-10 brokers, about 67% of individual F&O turnover. — [Business Standard, 25 Jan 2023](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/sebi-study-suggests-89-retail-traders-in-equity-f-o-suffered-losses-123012501466_1.html)
- **SEBI Sep 2024 study (FY22-FY24):** 93% (more than 1 crore) of individual F&O traders lost money. Aggregate losses exceeded ₹1.8 lakh crore over three years, including transaction costs. Only about 1% earned profits above ₹1 lakh after costs. — [AIBI copy of SEBI PR](https://aibi.org.in/Sebipr/Updated_SEBI_Study_Reveals_93_percentage_of_Individual_Traders_Incurred_Losses_in_Equity_F&O_between_FY22_and_FY24.pdf); [Business Standard, Sep 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/sebi-study-exposes-massive-losses-for-individual-f-o-traders-in-india-124092400948_1.html); [Zerodha TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/sebis-latest-analysis-of-profits-losses-in-the-equity-derivatives-segment-fy22-fy24/173596)

### Inferences
- Engine guardrail: exclude derivative, intraday and short-horizon technical or news signals by design. They carry a higher STCG rate (20%), STT and costs, and SEBI data shows more than 70-90% loss rates.
- An engine that "pings" users with frequent trade ideas risks raising trading frequency, which is the very behaviour Barber-Odean and SEBI's ">500 trades → 80% loss" data link to worse outcomes.

- No primary source on news/sentiment-driven retail trading outcomes in India.

## 6. Backtesting pitfalls and forward-testing a personal engine

### Takeaway
Most published return predictors are likely false positives or decay sharply. Harvey-Liu-Zhu set a t-stat hurdle of about 3.0 across 316 factors. McLean-Pontiff found 26% lower returns out-of-sample and 58% lower after publication. Indian factor-index histories are mostly pre-launch backtests. Indian frictions (STT, stamp duty, brokerage, 20% STCG, 12.5% LTCG) materially erode high-turnover strategies. An honest engine must be evaluated out-of-sample, after tax and cost, against a simple passive benchmark, with every signal logged at the time it was generated.

### Cited Findings
- **Harvey, Liu & Zhu (RFS 2016, 29(1):5-68):** catalogue 316 factors (313 published papers plus working papers). They argue a new factor needs a t-statistic above about 3.0, not 2.0, because of multiple testing. Given unpublished tests, 316 likely understates the factor population. — [Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P118_and_the_cross.PDF); [OUP RFS](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/29/1/5/1843824); [NBER WP 20592](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20592/w20592.pdf)
- **Counterpoint:** Chen et al. argue most claimed cross-sectional findings are likely true. The false-discovery debate is still open. — [arXiv 2206.15365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15365)
- **McLean & Pontiff (J. Finance 2016):** 97 predictors; 26% lower out-of-sample (an upper bound on data-mining bias) and 58% lower post-publication (about 32 pp attributable to informed trading). Bigger decay for stronger in-sample results. — [Ivey PDF](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf)
- **Survivorship correction:** the IIMA Indian factor library explicitly corrects returns for survival bias and excludes illiquid stocks. Engines using current-constituent lists or today's listed universe would overstate returns. — [IIMA data library](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/)
- **Fund survivorship:** SPIVA India's fund-level comparisons are survivorship-adjusted, and about 27% of funds did not survive 10 years to Dec 2025 (unverified secondary). — [S&P DJI SPIVA India](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india)
- **Indian taxes and costs:** STCG 20% and LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh — [Business Standard, Jul 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/budget-2024-hikes-ltcg-tax-rate-to-12-5-stcg-to-20-stt-on-f-o-also-up-124072300553_1.html). F&O STT increases from 1 Apr 2026 — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/securities-transaction-tax-stt). Trading costs added 57% on top of losses for losing intraday traders — [Business Standard, Jul 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)
- **Pre-launch history:** factor index history before launch is back-calculated (Alpha 50 base Dec 2003, launched Nov 2012). — [RupeeCase](https://www.rupeecase.com/guides/nifty-alpha-50)

### Inferences
Recommended evaluation protocol for the engine, derived from the above:
1. Freeze rules before testing. Record each recommendation with a timestamp, the data snapshot, and the expected-value rationale in an append-only log. This forward/paper-test removes look-ahead bias.
2. Benchmark against a "do-nothing" baseline: a direct Nifty 50/500 index fund plus the user's target debt allocation.
3. Score after tax (Indian rules from versioned per-tax-year data, per the AGENTS.md rule), after exit loads, STT, stamp duty and brokerage.
4. Count the number of distinct rules tried, and haircut results (HLZ-style t ≥ 3 or a deflated Sharpe).
5. Use point-in-time universes, including delisted stocks and merged or wound-up schemes.
6. Use at least one full cycle (5+ years) before claiming an edge. Momentum's 65-month recovery shows how long a factor can lag.

Classify opportunities by evidence tier:
- **Deterministic:** tax harvesting, direct plan, idle-cash sweep.
- **Statistical, long horizon:** passive over active, rebalancing.
- **Speculative:** factor timing, stock picks.

### Gaps
- No primary source retrieved for the delivery-equity STT (commonly 0.1% buy and sell), stamp duty (commonly 0.015% on buy), or SEBI/exchange fees. Verify against CBDT and the Indian Stamp Act notification before encoding.
- Bailey and López de Prado's "Probability of Backtest Overfitting" and "Deflated Sharpe Ratio" were not searched, so they are not cited here.
- No India-specific study quantifying look-ahead or survivorship bias in retail backtesting tools.

## 7. Behavioural evidence: checking and trading frequency vs returns

### Takeaway
Investors earn materially less than their funds. Globally, Morningstar puts the 10-year gap at about 1.2 pp/yr (about 15% of total return). In India, Axis MF studies show 13-14% investor returns vs 18-19% fund returns over two decades. The gap grows with trading frequency (Barber-Odean; SEBI's heavy-trader data). The size of the timing penalty is disputed: an FAJ 2026 paper argues it is about 0.10%/yr. An engine that reduces activity and enforces rules probably adds more than one that generates ideas.

### Cited Findings
- **Morningstar Mind the Gap 2025 (10 yrs to 31 Dec 2024, US):** dollar-weighted investor return was 7.0%/yr vs 8.2% fund total return, a 1.2 pp gap, about 15% of return. Bond-fund investors captured only about half their funds' returns. Allocation funds had a 0.1 pp gap. Headline: "The more investors traded, the less they made." — [Morningstar Mind the Gap 2025 PDF](https://www.morningstar.com/content/cs-assets/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/blt2c5c4d9171638c42/689b424311f3880edc4b4813/US_Mind_the_Gap_2025.pdf); [CNBC, 27 Oct 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/27/morningstar-research-investors-lose-out-on-mutual-fund-etf-returns.html)
- **Mind the Gap 2024 (10 yrs to 2023):** 6.3%/yr investor return, about 1.1 pp below fund returns. — [Morningstar Mind the Gap 2024](https://marketing.morningstar.com/content/cs-assets/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/blt5d1d34a59bd78a2c/680a4bd8c4d2e877fdb4b354/Mind_the_Gap_2024.pdf)
- **Later window (10 yrs to Dec 2025; secondhand, unverified against the Morningstar 2026 report):** investors 8.7% vs funds 9.9%. — [Advisor Perspectives/VettaFi, Aug 2026](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2026/08/21/what-advisors-can-learn-from-the-investor-return-gap)
- **Critique (Financial Analysts Journal, 2026):** "Bad Timing Does Not Cost Investors 15% of Their Funds' Returns". A replication gets a 17% "return gap" but concludes poor timing costs only about 0.10%/yr. The rest is a measurement artefact of flows into growing markets. Morningstar itself says regular contributions and rebalancing can open a gap. — [FAJ via CFA Institute RPC](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2026/bad-timing-does-not-cost-investors-funds-returns); [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0015198X.2026.2657253)
- **India (Axis MF):**
  - 2003-2020: average equity investor 13.2% vs equity SIP 14.5% vs equity funds 18.7%. Attributed to short-termism, market timing and churn. — [Business Standard, 1 Apr 2021](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pf/returns-made-by-average-mf-investor-lag-those-of-schemes-and-sips-study-121040101236_1.html)
  - 2003-2022: funds 19.1% vs investors 13.8%. — [Business Standard/ANI](https://www.business-standard.com/content/press-releases-ani/decoding-wealth-creation-expert-analysis-on-the-behaviour-gap-in-investment-returns-vs-investor-returns-by-santosh-kedari-ceo-sanriya-finvest-123082900013_1.html)
- **Trading frequency:** Barber & Odean (2000) found the most active households earned 11.4% vs 18.5% for infrequent traders. — [Odean PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/Papers%20current%20versions/Individual_Investor_Performance_Final.pdf). SEBI FY23 data shows 80% of intraday traders with more than 500 trades/yr lost money, vs 71% overall. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html)

### Inferences
- In India, systematic SIP investing captured more of fund returns than discretionary investing (14.5% vs 13.2% in Axis 2003-2020). The engine should nudge toward automated SIPs and rule-based rebalancing, and away from discretionary timing.
- The return-gap literature is contested on magnitude (FAJ 2026). Product copy should not claim "users lose 15%". The defensible claim is that frequent trading and chasing hurt returns (Barber-Odean, SEBI).
- Design implication: notification cadence should be low, for example monthly or quarterly, and tied to rule triggers (band breach, tax deadline, idle cash above threshold), not to market moves.

### Gaps
- No study found linking portfolio-checking frequency to returns for Indian investors. Myopic loss aversion literature (Benartzi-Thaler) was not searched.
- No post-2022 Indian investor-return-gap study (AMFI/SEBI/AMC) was found. The Axis studies are the latest retrieved, and the 2003-2022 figure comes from a press release.
- Primary Axis MF study documents were not retrieved; only press coverage was.
