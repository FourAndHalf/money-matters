Invest engine: build plan

How to turn the Invest tab into an engine that checks the whole portfolio every day for opportunities, ranks them in after-tax rupees, interrupts you only when something has a deadline, and records what you decided. It builds on reports/Invest tab recommendation engine.md and the research notes behind it. It also extends docs/plan.html and docs/invest-plan.md.

Status: proposal, 29 Oct 2026. Nothing here is a settled decision until it is recorded in docs/plan.html (Phase 0). This is a design for a personal tool, not financial advice.

Summary

The engine is a deterministic rule enforcer on the device. Each rule reads the ledger, the lots, prices, deposits, SIPs, goals and a signed investment policy. Each rule either produces an opportunity (a typed record with rupee amounts in Paise, inputs with as-of dates, and the rule that fired) or stays silent. Opportunities are ranked by expected after-tax benefit minus cost and sorted into three alert classes. The app suggests the action, hands off to your broker or platform to carry it out, and records the trade in the books when you confirm it. It never places orders.

45 opportunities, 7 families

Contribution and cash, allocation and risk, cost, tax, deposits and bonds, discipline, and execution helpers. A separate list of 8 signals is excluded on purpose.

19 can start with no schema change

Phase 1 uses functions that already exist: drift, concentrations, harvestRoom, taxIfSoldToday, equityYears, maturityLadder, sipInstalments and lockUntil.

The biggest lever is contribution

₹1 Cr by 20 Nov 2029 needs about ₹2.33–2.44L a month. Measuring and steering how much you can invest each month (C1, C2) is worth more than every cost and tax rule put together.

Six phases

0 Decide → 1 Advisor v1 → 2 Memory and goals → 3 Daily loop → 4 Universe and narration → 5 Explicit add-ons. Each slice is its own feature/ branch with tests.

Principles

  1. Rules, not forecasts. The engine acts on cost, tax and discipline, where the payoff is near-certain. It never predicts prices. Evidence: SPIVA India YE2025 (75–84% of active large-cap funds trail over 1–10 years), factor drawdowns of 68–79%, McLean–Pontiff decay of 58% after publication, LLM extrapolation bias, and SEBI's F&O loss studies.
  2. Numbers in code, words from the model. Every amount is integer Paise computed in Kotlin from versioned data. An optional LLM only narrates, and its numbers are checked against the record.
  3. Suggest and record, never place orders. SEBI's retail algo framework (in force 1 Apr 2026) requires a static IP and a 2FA login every day for API orders. Mutual fund and FD booking have no individual API. Automation means mandates and broker-side orders that you set up once. The app reminds you and reconciles.
  4. Check everything, interrupt rarely. All rules run on every holding every day. Push notifications go out only for deadlines, and within a budget. A price move alone never triggers a push (FCA: notifications raised trading by about 11%; Robinhood alerts by about 25% in the next 15 minutes).
  5. Prove it ran and prove it was right. Every scan is logged, fixtures must keep firing, and every proposal is scored against doing nothing (lessons from Betterment's $9M SEC case and Knight Capital).
  6. Personal use only. Recommendations for yourself are outside the SEBI Investment Adviser and Research Analyst rules. Publishing them, charging for them or sharing them as a strategy is not.
  7. AGENTS.md holds. Balanced entries, integer paise, immutable closed months, tax figures in versioned per-year data, tests for any tax logic, and UI built from MoneyTheme tokens and ui/components.

What "relentless" means here

On Android, "relentless" can't mean scanning more often. WorkManager's 15-minute floor is only a suggestion. The "rare" standby bucket allows 10 minutes of jobs a day, and on Android 13+ an app unused for 8 days becomes "restricted" and gets one job a day. Nothing in the catalogue changes faster than daily: NAVs publish once a day, FD rates monthly, tax deadlines yearly. So relentless means four guarantees:

GuaranteeHow it is metPhase
Coverage: every rule, every holding, every dayOne daily AdviceWorker run, chained after any price refresh. The Invest tab also runs the engine live whenever it opens.1 (live), 3 (daily)
No missed deadlinesDeadline-class alerts escalate on a schedule: 31 Mar harvest window, FD maturity, carry-forward expiry, GTT expiry.3
Proof of lifeA scan_run log, a "Scanned today" badge that turns to the warning colour after 3 missed days, and bundled self-test fixtures run on every scan.3
Proof of valueA decision journal scores accepted and ignored proposals at 30, 90 and 365 days against doing nothing.2

An Invest home-screen widget (beside the three existing Glance widgets) shows the top open opportunity and the last scan date. Apps with an active widget are exempt from Android's restricted bucket, so the widget also keeps the daily job reliable.

Architecture

The engine follows the existing layering: pure math in core/invest, state and screens in feature/invest. It also follows the plan's insights pipeline: "Ledger → Rules engine (on device, deterministic) → Facts JSON → AI → Weekly summary" (plan.html:432).

  • Package: core/invest/advice/ with Opportunity.kt (types), Policy.kt (IPS), Scoring.kt, Rules*.kt grouped by family (RulesContribution, RulesAllocation, RulesCost, RulesTax, RulesDeposits, RulesDiscipline), and Engine.kt (opportunities()).

  • Reuse, not rewrite: rules call valuations, allocation, drift, concentrations, taxIfSoldToday, equityYears, depositValuation, maturityLadder (moved from feature/invest to core/invest), sipInstalments and requiredMonthlyContribution (moved from feature/dashboard to core).

Opportunity record

enum class OpportunityType { C1, C2, …, E4 }          // catalogue IDs
enum class EvidenceTier { Deterministic, Statistical, Inform }
enum class ApprovalTier { T0, T1, T2 }
enum class AlertClass { Deadline, Digest, Quiet }

data class Opportunity(
    val key: String,                 // stable identity, e.g. "T1:2026-27", "F1:fd-42"
    val type: OpportunityType,
    val subject: Subject,            // Portfolio, Holding(id), Deposit(id), Sip(id), Goal(id)
    val benefit: Paise,              // expected after-tax benefit; Zero for Inform
    val cost: Paise,                 // tax now + exit load + charges
    val evidence: EvidenceTier,
    val deadline: LocalDate?,
    val action: ProposedAction,      // legs with Paise and Units; never Double
    val tier: ApprovalTier,
    val alert: AlertClass,
    val inputs: List<InputFact>,     // label, value, asOf, priceStatus
    val rule: RuleRef,               // rule id, IPS version and clause, tax-data version
    val ifIgnored: Paise?,           // cost of doing nothing, where computable
)
  • key lets the daily scan update an existing proposal instead of creating a duplicate, and lets dismissals and mutes stick.
  • ProposedAction mirrors the repository calls it would book once confirmed: sell, buy, orderFund, openDeposit, closeDeposit(renew), SIP change. A confirmed action goes through those calls, so every entry still balances.
  • All card text is a template filled from the record's fields, so the text can't disagree with the numbers.

Scoring and ranking

  1. Net benefit = benefit − cost, in paise.
  2. Confidence by evidence tier, in basis points: Deterministic 10,000; Statistical 6,000 (default); Inform has no rupee score.
  3. Score = net × confidenceBps / 10,000, rounded half-up with BigInteger, the same way as proRata.
  4. Materiality floor: drop anything with score < ₹500 (default, IPS). Hygiene items (D1–D3, D6, F1, T4) are exempt.
  5. Policy filter: drop or downgrade anything that would breach the IPS (turnover cap, minimum holding period, banned instruments, caps).
  6. Order: Deadline class first, by deadline. Then Digest by score. Quiet items are listed but never ranked into the digest's top 5.

"Do nothing" is a first-class option on every card, with ifIgnored shown when it can be computed (for example the T1 tax you'd pay later, or K1's yearly TER drag).

Phase 2 adds a learning step: per-type confidence is adjusted from the journal's realised-versus-estimated ratio, capped within ±20% and logged as a policy-version change.

Liveness, paper mode and the decision journal

  • Scan log: each run writes scan_run (started, finished, rules version, policy version, tax-data version, opportunities found, self-test result, error).
  • Self-test: a small bundled fixture portfolio with known answers runs on every scan. If any expected opportunity fails to fire, D6 fires.
  • Badge: "Scanned today" on the Invest module and widget; warning colour after 3 days without a run.
  • Kill switch: one toggle pauses every proposal and alert. It trips by itself on ledger imbalance, a failed self-test or 3 consecutive failed price downloads.
  • Paper mode: for the first 8 weeks, proposals go to a "Paper" list with no pushes, and the journal records what would have happened.
  • Journal: every proposal is stored with its input snapshot and score. Your decision (Accepted, Snoozed, Dismissed with reason, Expired) is recorded. At 30, 90 and 365 days, the realised result is compared with doing nothing, using the same price and tax functions. The journal is append-only.
  • Review screen: per type, how often it fired, how often you accepted, estimated vs realised rupees. A rule that doesn't earn its keep after a year is a candidate for removal.

AI narration (optional, Phase 4)

  • Opt-in through the existing AiSettingsStore (your key, payload preview). Template text is always the fallback.
  • Input: one Opportunity record as JSON, with account names and identifiers removed (aggregates only, plan.html:432-437).
  • Output: 2–4 sentences on "why this, why now, what could go wrong", as structured output separate from any numbers.
  • Validator: extract every number and percent from the text; reject and fall back to the template if any isn't in the record within rounding.
  • Store the text with model ID and prompt version.
  • Cost: Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens (claude.com/pricing, 29 Oct 2026). A weekly digest of 10 cards costs cents a year. Don't budget from Haiku 4.5 ($1/$5), which is ten times more.
  • Never: generating ideas, choosing amounts, or seeing raw ledger rows.

  • Tax: T1, T2 and T8 against hand-worked cases for both 2018 and 2024 rule sets; changing the data file changes the answer without code changes.
