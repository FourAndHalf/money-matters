# Make the Invest tab enforce rules, not predict

The sound way to turn Money Matters' Invest tab into a recommendation engine is to build a **deterministic, on-device rule enforcer that scans every day, interrupts rarely, and never places orders on its own**. It should hunt for cost, tax and discipline opportunities, not for stocks to buy. Those opportunities are regular-to-direct plan switches, new money steered to the underweight asset class, the ₹1.25 lakh equity LTCG exemption before 31 March, idle cash above the emergency target, FD maturities and deposit-insurance concentration, missed SIPs and ELSS lock-ins ending. The evidence splits sharply along this line. Most active Indian large-cap funds trail their benchmark over 3 to 10 years. Factor strategies that look strong in backtests have suffered drawdowns of 55 to 79%. LLM return forecasts carry known biases. Frequent trading and push alerts reliably make retail outcomes worse. India's execution rails point the same way. Since 1 April 2026, SEBI's retail algo framework requires a whitelisted static IP and a daily 2FA login for API orders. Broker APIs cannot place mutual fund orders. Individuals cannot become Account Aggregator data users. So in practice "automation" means mandates and broker-side orders set up once, plus an app that recommends, records and reconciles. The codebase is unusually ready for this: the lot, FIFO and tax engine, drift, concentration, harvest room, FD ladder and SIP states already exist as pure functions, so version 1 needs no schema change. What is missing is stored goals, an investable-surplus figure, an instrument master, notification plumbing and a decision journal. "Relentless" should mean never missing a daily scan, and being able to prove each one ran. It should not mean scanning more often. This is a design analysis for a personal tool, not financial advice. Several tax and regulatory facts below rest on secondary sources and must be checked against primary texts before they go into code or data files.

## Cost, tax and discipline rules beat return forecasts on the evidence

The strongest evidence favours actions whose payoff is close to certain. In **SPIVA India Year-End 2025**, active large-cap funds underperformed the S&P BSE 100 **75.0% of the time over 1 year, 84.4% over 5 years and 76.3% over 10 years**, and a firm majority of funds in every category trailed over the decade ([SPIVA India YE2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-india-scorecard-year-end-2025.pdf); [S&P DJI SPIVA India](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india)). One site gives an 82.4% 10-year large-cap figure. It conflicts with S&P's own 76.3%, so use S&P's number. An Indian adviser also argues that the post-2024 benchmark choice overstates large-cap underperformance ([BasuNivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/active-vs-passive-why-you-must-not-trust-spiva-india-report/)). Mid and small-cap active funds did deliver majority outperformance in calendar 2025, so a rule of "always switch to index" is weaker outside large caps. The more certain saving is the plan type. Regular plans cost roughly **0.65 to 1.0 percentage points a year more than direct plans** in active equity ([PrimeInvestor](https://primeinvestor.in/reports/mf-direct-vs-regular-plans-an-expense-ratio-guide/); [Rupaywise](https://rupaywise.com/guides/direct-vs-regular-mutual-funds)), and a 2013–2023 study found direct plans ahead by about 1 to 1.5% a year ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392652036_Risk-Adjusted_Performance_Comparison_of_Direct_and_Regular_Plan_Equity_Mutual_Funds_in_India)). The PrimeInvestor figure dates from around 2023. A December 2025 SEBI expense-ratio reform reported by one blog was not confirmed against a SEBI source.

The tax rules create India's own version of "harvesting". Equity LTCG is taxed at **12.5% above a ₹1.25 lakh yearly exemption**, and STCG on holdings of 12 months or less at **20%**. Both rates were unchanged by Budget 2026 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/budget-2024-hikes-ltcg-tax-rate-to-12-5-stcg-to-20-stt-on-f-o-also-up-124072300553_1.html); [Tata MF](https://www.tatamutualfund.com/blogs/post-budget-2026-new-tax-map-equity-investors)). Booking up to ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains each financial year and rebuying raises the cost basis tax-free. That saves up to about **₹15,625 of future tax a year per PAN** ([Strike](https://www.strike.money/stock-market/ltcg-tax)). It is the inverse of US tax-loss harvesting, and its payoff is computable to the rupee. For a 30%-slab investor with money parked for more than 12 months, an arbitrage fund (taxed as equity) nets about 6.1% against about 4.9% for a liquid fund in one illustrative 7% case ([Bajaj AMC](https://www.bajajamc.com/knowledge-centre/arbitrage-fund-taxation)). Debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rate whatever the holding period. On rebalancing, Vanguard's research finds no material risk-adjusted difference between monthly and annual rebalancing. Annual or semi-annual checks with **5-point bands** balance risk control against cost ([Vanguard](https://corpaemdisp.essp.c1.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/articles/tuning-frequency-for-rebalancing.html); [WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/investment/art-rebalancing)). That matches the ±5% drift alert the app already has.

The speculative end of the spectrum looks much worse once costs and honesty are applied. Indian factor indices have impressive long-run backtests, but Nifty200 Momentum 30 has a reported **68% maximum drawdown** ([NSE Indices whitepaper](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/momentum-strategy-whitepaper_2026.pdf)). One independent backtest shows a 65-month recovery ([BacktestIndia](https://backtestindia.com/blog/momentum-investing-india-backtest)). Factor leadership flipped over the five years to August 2026: Value 30 returned 26% a year against 9% for Quality 30 ([RupeeCase](https://www.rupeecase.com/learn/path-3/module-3-8-factor-investing-india-evidence)). Published return predictors lose **26% out of sample and 58% after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf)). New factors need a t-statistic of about 3.0 to survive multiple testing ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P118_and_the_cross.PDF)). LLMs are no shortcut. GPT-4o return forecasts **over-extrapolate recent performance and are over-optimistic**, even on samples after its training data ([Chen et al.](https://www.ualberta.ca/en/finance-department/media-library/llm-bias-green.pdf)). LLMs also memorise historical market data, which spoils naive backtests. No India-specific study of LLM stock-picking was found. At the far end, SEBI's own studies found **93% of individual F&O traders lost money over FY22–24** ([SEBI via AIBI](https://aibi.org.in/Sebipr/Updated_SEBI_Study_Reveals_93_percentage_of_Individual_Traders_Incurred_Losses_in_Equity_F&O_between_FY22_and_FY24.pdf)) and **71% of intraday traders lost money in FY23** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/7-in-10-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-suffered-losses-in-fy23-sebi-study-124072400975_1.html)).

Behaviour is the final filter. The most active US households earned **11.4% a year against 18.5% for infrequent traders** ([Barber & Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/Papers%20current%20versions/Individual_Investor_Performance_Final.pdf)). In India, Axis MF found equity SIPs captured 14.5% a year against 13.2% for the average equity investor over 2003–2020 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pf/returns-made-by-average-mf-investor-lag-those-of-schemes-and-sips-study-121040101236_1.html)). In an FCA experiment, push notifications alone raised trading frequency by **about 11%** ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/research-notes/research-note-digital-engagement-practices-trading-apps-experiment.pdf); figure via search summary, not fetched). The size of the investor "return gap" is contested. Morningstar puts it at 1.2 points a year, while a 2026 *Financial Analysts Journal* paper argues that poor timing costs only about 0.10% a year ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/content/cs-assets/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/blt2c5c4d9171638c42/689b424311f3880edc4b4813/US_Mind_the_Gap_2025.pdf); [FAJ](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2026/bad-timing-does-not-cost-investors-funds-returns)). The defensible conclusion is not "you lose 15% to bad timing". It is that **an engine that reduces activity and enforces rules adds more than one that generates ideas**.

One fact puts every rule above in proportion. The plan's own arithmetic says the "₹1 Crore by 30" goal (20 Nov 2029) needs a monthly SIP of about **₹2.33–2.44 lakh** at 7–10% returns ([plan.html:395-399](../docs/plan.html)). A 1-point fee saving or a well-timed harvest cannot close a gap of that size. Only the contribution amount can. So the engine's most valuable output is an honest monthly answer to "how much can I invest, and where should it go?" That figure (the plan's "investable X") is still unmeasured ([plan.html:712](../docs/plan.html)).

| Opportunity | Evidence tier | Payoff | Inputs in code today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular → direct switch, net of exit load and tax | Deterministic | ~0.65–1.0 pp/yr | No: needs scheme master and expense-ratio data |
| ₹1.25L LTCG gain harvest (Feb–Mar) | Deterministic | ≤ ~₹15,625/yr | Yes: `harvestRoom`, `taxIfSoldToday` |
| New money to underweight class | Statistical, long horizon | Risk control, fewer taxable sells | Yes: `allocation`, `drift` |
| Idle cash above 9× essential spend | Deterministic | Cash-drag removal | Partly: balances and essential categories exist; ₹5L target is a plan constant |
| FD maturing / DICGC ₹5L per bank | Deterministic | Rate gap, insurance cover | Partly: `maturityLadder`; no bank field, no rate data |
| Missed SIP, ELSS lock ending, concentration breach | Discipline | Behavioural | Yes: `sipInstalments`, `lockUntil`, `concentrations` |
| Factor rotation, stock picks, LLM ideas, F&O | Speculative / negative | Unreliable or harmful | Exclude by design |

## India's rails allow recommending and recording, not unattended execution

**Personal use is legally clean; distribution is not.** The Investment Adviser Regulations apply to advice given for consideration, and advice given without any consideration is not "investment advice" under them ([Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/default.aspx?id=5966)). The Research Analyst Regulations, amended on **16 Dec 2024** to add "research services" and "trading calls", can capture published buy/sell/hold calls even when they are free ([AZB Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/overhaul-of-the-regulatory-framework-for-investment-advisers-and-research-analysts/); [AlgoTest](https://algotest.in/blog/research-analysts-demystified-what-your-finfluencer-needs-to-do-now-sebi/)). No explicit family exemption was found in either regime. An engine that serves only its owner is outside both. Publishing its picks, charging for the app, or shipping it as a "strategy" for others would plausibly bring it inside. The researchers could not read the exemption clauses verbatim, so a free tool shared with non-dependent relatives remains a grey zone.

**Order automation runs into SEBI's retail algo framework.** The circular of 4 Feb 2025 has applied to all brokers since **1 April 2026** ([AlgoBulls](https://algobulls.com/blog/industry-insights-and-updates/sebi-new-algotrading-regulations-for-retail-investors-2026); [Fyers notice](https://fyers.in/notice-board/new-sebi-framework-for-retail-algo-trading-from-april-01-2026)). A self-coded algo under about **10 orders per second** needs no exchange registration. Its orders still must come from a **whitelisted static IP** (one primary, one backup, changeable once a week), sessions must be logged out daily so **2FA is needed every trading day**, and market orders need non-zero market protection ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/general-kite/kite-api/articles/static-ip); [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/investing-guide/articles/retail-algo-trading-rules-nse-circular/)). Only order endpoints are IP-checked; holdings and quotes work from any IP. A phone on a carrier network has no stable IP, so placing orders from the app would need a self-hosted relay on a static IP. That breaks the plan's "no backend" decision ([plan.html:163](../docs/plan.html)) and adds attack surface, all to save a few taps a month. Sources disagree on whether a static IP may be shared within a family: some vendor explainers say yes with broker approval ([TrueData](https://www.truedata.in/blog/new-nse-rules-on-retail-algo-trading-what-traders-platforms-and-brokers-need-to-know)), while a Zerodha forum reply says no on Kite ([Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/52028/)). The exact threshold wording (≥10 or >10 orders per second, per exchange or per client) is unverified against the NSE circular.

**Mutual funds, FDs and account data have no individual API at all.** Kite Connect's mutual fund endpoints are read-only, because placement needs payment from the user's bank ([Kite MF docs](https://kite.trade/docs/connect/v3/mutual-funds/)). BSE StAR MF access is granted only to members and vendors ([BSE StAR MF API](https://www.bsestarmf.in/APIFileStructure.pdf)). An Account Aggregator data user must be a regulated entity certified through Sahamati ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/fiu/)). No retail FD-booking API was found; digital FDs run on B2B infrastructure such as Upswing. Deposit insurance is still **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank** on pages updated in October 2026 ([Paisabazaar](https://www.paisabazaar.com/fixed-deposit/)), but no primary DICGC source was fetched. That leaves three automation primitives that need no app-held credential: **SIPs on eNACH or UPI AutoPay mandates** (UPI AutoPay allows up to ₹1 lakh per MF debit without re-authentication) ([Zerodha TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/introducing-upi-autopay-for-sip-payments/173064)); **broker-side GTT orders**, valid for a year and cancelled by corporate actions ([Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/tradezerodha/kite/introducing-gtt-good-till-triggered-orders)); and **CAS statements** from CAMS, KFintech or the depositories for reconciliation ([AMFI CAS](https://www.amfiindia.com/investor-corner/investor-center/cas.html)).

**Broker pricing conflicts across the notes.** The data-sources note puts Kite Connect's data tier at **₹2,000/month**, citing coverage from February to April 2025 ([Marketcalls](https://www.marketcalls.in/fintech/zerodha-makes-trading-api-free-for-personal-use-bundles-historical-data-with-connect-api.html)). The regulation note puts it at **₹500/month**, citing Zerodha's own charges page and a forum thread about cutting the fee from ₹2,000 to ₹500 ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/kite-web-and-mobile/kite-api/articles/what-are-the-charges-for-kite-apis); [Kite forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/comment/49597)). **₹500 is the more recent and more credible figure.** It describes a cut from the ₹2,000 the 2025 articles report, it comes from a Zerodha page, and Groww made a parallel cut from ₹2,000 to ₹499 ([Marketcalls on Groww](https://www.marketcalls.in/fintech/groww-enters-the-algo-arena-with-powerful-trading-apis.html)). Neither researcher fetched the page directly, so confirm before subscribing. Order APIs at Zerodha, Dhan and Angel are free. Upstox's ₹10-per-order API fee was reported as running to 31 Mar 2026 by an older comparison and to 31 Dec 2026 by Upstox staff posts from October 2026. The later date is more current ([OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/trading-apis/algo-trading-charges)). None of this matters for version 1, which needs no broker data feed.

**The app already sits on a data-terms fault line.** The existing `PriceUpdater` downloads the NSE bhavcopy from `nsearchives.nseindia.com` with a browser User-Agent and an NSE Referer ([PriceFetcher.kt:35-95](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/PriceFetcher.kt); [PriceUpdater.kt:17-41](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/PriceUpdater.kt)). NSE's terms prohibit "any systematic or automated data collection activities" and redistribution without permission ([NSE Terms of Use](https://www.nseindia.com/static/nse-terms-of-use)). One weekly file for personal use is low-risk, but it is not clean, and the researcher's claim that the archive host falls under the same terms is unverified. Do not make it worse by polling NSE pages more often, and never let NSE-derived data feed anything shared. AMFI's `NAVAll.txt` is the clean, free daily source for mutual funds. Its reuse terms were not checked either.

**Tax data must straddle two Acts.** The Income-tax Act 2025 replaced the 1961 Act on **1 April 2026** with a single "tax year" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income-tax_Act,_2025)). Secondary sources map s.111A to **s.196**, s.112A to **s.198** and 80C to **s.123** ([Ebizfiling](https://ebizfiling.com/blog/section-196-and-198-short-term-and-long-term-capital-gains/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/income-tax-act-2025-section-80c-becomes-section-123-from-april-1-2026-heres-what-taxpayers-must-know-522120-2026-03-24)). The app's `capital_gains.json` has only 2018 and 2024 entries and is marked "unverified against the Income-tax Act 2025" ([invest-plan.md:118](../docs/invest-plan.md)). Three further points are unresolved: whether the debt-fund ("specified MF") redefinition took effect on 1 Apr 2025 or 1 Apr 2026, the new-Act home of the 31 Jan 2018 grandfathering rule, and the set-off rules for harvested losses. ELSS deductions exist only under the old regime ([Tata Capital](https://www.tatacapital.com/blog/wealth-services/elss-funds/)). The profile already stores `taxRegime`, so the engine should suppress ELSS-for-tax suggestions for new-regime users.

## The codebase already holds most of the engine's inputs

The Invest module is a strong base. `core/invest` has pure, date-parameterised functions for `allocation`, `drift` (5-point alert), `concentrations` (10% of equity), `taxIfSoldToday` and `capitalGainsRegister` ([Analytics.kt:10-143](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/Analytics.kt)). Next to these sit `harvestRoom` and the FD `maturityLadder` ([InvestState.kt:292-302](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/feature/invest/InvestState.kt)), SIP instalment states with missed-SIP goal impact ([Sip.kt:6-61](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/Sip.kt)), per-lot `lockUntil` dates and `requiredMonthlyContribution` ([DashboardMath.kt:54](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/feature/dashboard/DashboardMath.kt)). They all feed one combined state flow, `buildInvest` ([InvestViewModel.kt:76](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/feature/invest/InvestViewModel.kt)). The plan already defines the right pipeline: "Ledger → Rules engine (on device, deterministic) → Facts JSON → AI → Weekly summary", where the AI sees aggregates only and "never does the math" ([plan.html:432-437](../docs/plan.html)). It also names the policies the engine would enforce: rebalance with new money first, the emergency fund before the wealth goal, and goal-horizon allocation bands ([plan.html:348](../docs/plan.html); [plan.html:380-408](../docs/plan.html)). The engine is therefore a new pure module, `core/invest/advice`, shaped like `Analytics.kt`. It returns typed `Opportunity` records carrying rupee amounts in `Paise`, the inputs with their as-of dates and price badges, the rule that fired, and the cost of doing nothing. Those records feed `InvestState` and later P19's Facts JSON.

Five gaps block the fuller version. First, **there is no goal, budget, recurring-rule or insight table**. Goals are two booleans in an encrypted profile plus a hard-coded 12% return, and `SipPlanEntity.goalId` points at nothing ([MoneyMattersDatabase.kt:67-72](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/ledger/MoneyMattersDatabase.kt); [MainActivity.kt:92](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/MainActivity.kt)). Second, **there is no instrument master**. `parseAmfi` reads the whole market's scheme codes and then discards every row except held ISINs ([PriceFeeds.kt:14-92](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/PriceFeeds.kt); [PriceUpdater.kt:97-135](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/PriceUpdater.kt)). That is why regular-to-direct detection and index alternatives are impossible today, even though the data arrives every week. Third, **thresholds are code constants**: the drift band, the concentration limit, `STALE_DAYS`, `SIP_GRACE_DAYS`, and an `ELSS_LOCK_YEARS` tax rule that AGENTS.md requires to live in versioned data ([Analytics.kt:31-32](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/Analytics.kt); [InvestRepository.kt:733-736](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/InvestRepository.kt)). Fourth, **there are no notifications**: no channel, no `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` permission, and the only background job is the weekly `PriceWorker` ([AndroidManifest.xml:4-59](../app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml); [PriceWorker.kt:18-52](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/PriceWorker.kt)). Fifth, **the database is at version 12, but exported schemas stop at `11.json`**, so the schema export needs fixing before the next migration.

"Relentless" has a hard ceiling on Android, and the opportunities do not need more than that ceiling allows. WorkManager's 15-minute minimum is "a suggestion" under Doze. Apps in the "rare" standby bucket get 10 minutes of jobs per 24 hours, and on Android 13+ an app untouched for 8 days drops to "restricted", which allows one job a day ([CommonsWare](https://commonsware.com/AndroidArch/pages/chap-workmgr-014); [Android power limits](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details)). Every opportunity in the table above changes on a daily or slower clock: NAVs publish once a day, FD rates monthly, and tax deadlines yearly. So a **once-daily on-device scan is enough**. Android exempts apps with active widgets from the restricted bucket ([App Standby Buckets](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/appstandby)), and Money Matters already ships three Glance widgets ([AndroidManifest.xml:8-59](../app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml)). An "Invest" widget row showing the top open opportunity and the last scan date would both surface signals and keep the daily job reliable. Prices can stay weekly by default, with a forced refresh when the user opens a harvest or rebalance card, so no action is ever approved on a stale price. The plan already requires that "a failure is shown, never hidden behind an old price" ([invest-plan.md:87](../docs/invest-plan.md)).

The data-sources researcher proposed a different design for "always-on": a free GitHub Actions or Cloudflare cron backend that scans public data and wakes the phone with a high-priority FCM message, while the ledger stays on the phone. It is a sound design, but it belongs late in the roadmap. It earns its place only for **intraday-relevant events on individual stocks**, such as results, insider deals and corporate actions from NSE's official RSS feeds ([NSE RSS](https://www.nseindia.com/static/rss-feed)), and the evidence says the user should not be acting on those within hours. Adopting it means reversing a recorded "no backend" decision, so defer it until a concrete stock-event need appears.

The LLM's place is narrator, never signal generator. The plan's opt-in, user-key, aggregates-only design and its payload preview fit this already, and `AiSettingsStore` exists but is unused ([AiSettingsStore.kt:7-38](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/feature/settings/AiSettingsStore.kt)). The pattern is to pass each `Opportunity` record as JSON, ask for two to four sentences on "why this, why now, what could go wrong", then reject any output containing a number not present in the input. Cost is trivial. The data note's price table, taken from claude.com/pricing on 29 Oct 2026, lists **Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 per million input/output tokens** (Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10, Opus 5.5 $4/$20), with batch at 50% off ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). That matches the Anthropic API reference bundled with Claude Code (cached 6 Oct 2026), so the notes do not actually conflict here. The real risk is a stale assumption: the previous-generation Haiku 4.5 costs $1/$5, ten times more, and an estimate built on it would be off by an order of magnitude. Even so, summarising 50 filings a day comes to under $1 a month on Haiku 5.5, and about $10 on Haiku 4.5. Narrating a weekly digest of ten cards costs cents a year.

## Guardrails: a signed policy, a quiet inbox and proof the scanner ran

**Write the investment policy statement (IPS) as code** and give it a versioned, user-signed object that the engine must satisfy before proposing anything. The object holds target allocation and bands (moving today's constants into it), per-stock and per-sector caps, the emergency floor of 9× essential spend, a materiality floor such as ₹500 net benefit, maximum yearly turnover, a minimum holding period that avoids 20% STCG churn, and a hard ban on F&O, margin and intraday. Changes to the IPS should take effect only after a cooling-off period, for example 72 hours. Rank opportunities by expected after-tax rupee benefit, minus costs, times confidence, adjusted for deadline urgency. Show "do nothing" as a first-class option on every card. Copy two industry patterns that cut taxable events: use cash flows first, as Betterment does ([Betterment](https://betterment.com/help/portfolio-rebalancing)), and rebalance only partway back to target, as in Vanguard's 200 bp trigger with a 175 bp destination ([Vanguard](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/the_rebalancing_edge_optimizing_target_date_fund_rebalancing_through_threshold_based_strategies.pdf)). Every sell in India is a capital-gains event, so these matter more here than in the US. Betterment's own pages give conflicting default bands (3% vs 5% for mutual fund portfolios), which is one more reason to keep the band a user setting.

**Use approval tiers that match India's rails.** Tier 0 covers what already runs without a click: SIP instalments on existing mandates and FD auto-renewals. The app records and reconciles these, as it already does with SIP proposals and In-Transit allotment. Tier 1 is one-tap approval for harvests, in-band rebalances, FD reinvestments and direct-plan switches. The app opens the user's platform through a deep link and books the trade with the existing `buy`, `sell` or `orderFund` repository calls once the user confirms ([InvestRepository.kt](../app/src/main/java/com/moneymatters/app/core/invest/InvestRepository.kt)). Tier 2 needs a written rationale, which fills the "investment reason" field the plan asks for but `TransactionEntity` lacks ([plan.html:311](../docs/plan.html)). It applies to adding a new stock, breaching a cap or selling at a large tax cost. Leverage, derivatives, new mandates and IPS changes are never automatable. Do not store a TOTP seed to automate the daily broker login. That collapses SEBI-mandated 2FA into a single factor.

**Run a quiet inbox.** Batch non-urgent items into the weekly summary the plan already specifies. Interrupt immediately only for deadline-bound items, such as an FD maturing within days, the harvest window closing on 31 March, or a GTT expiring. Never push because a price moved, and never use streaks, confetti or "you missed X%" framing. The FCA and Robinhood evidence ([Iowa dissertation](https://iro.uiowa.edu/view/pdfCoverPage?instCode=01IOWA_INST&filePid=13873804220002771&download=true)) shows these features raise trading, which is the behaviour the evidence links to worse returns. DESIGN.md's rule that colour appears only for meaning, "small: a delta, a badge, a dot", already enforces the right tone ([DESIGN.md:175-177](../design/DESIGN.md)).

**Prove the scanner is alive and right.** Betterment paid a **$9M SEC penalty** after an undisclosed change in scan frequency and coding errors silently stopped tax-loss harvesting for more than 25,000 accounts ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/19/robo-advisor-betterment-settles-tax-charges-with-sec-for-9-million.html)). Knight Capital lost about **$461M in 45 minutes** when dormant code reactivated ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001060749/000119312512513607/d457296dex21.htm)). For a personal app, the countermeasures are cheap. Keep an append-only log of every scan and every proposal with its input snapshot. Show a "last scanned" badge that turns to the warning colour after 3 missed days. Maintain a fixture suite of known opportunities that must keep firing, which AGENTS.md already requires for tax logic. Keep a decision journal that scores accepted and ignored proposals at 30, 90 and 365 days against doing nothing. Run in paper mode for the first several weeks, so the journal records what the engine would have suggested before the user relies on it. That journal is also the only honest way to judge whether a rule earns its place.

## Conclusion

Asking the Invest tab to "find opportunities" turns out to be mostly a request to make the app's existing promises executable. The highest-value findings, such as unused LTCG room, money that should go to the underweight class, cash that belongs in the emergency fund, and a regular plan quietly costing a percentage point a year, are all computable from data the app either holds or discards every Saturday. None of them depends on predicting a price. The most consequential change is reframing "relentless" from frequency to reliability: one daily scan, an inbox that stays quiet, and a ledger of what the engine proposed and what came of it. That design passes AGENTS.md's rules almost for free, because every recommendation is integer-paise arithmetic over versioned tax data with tests.

The uncomfortable implication sits outside the Invest tab. Against a ₹1 crore target that needs roughly ₹2.3 lakh a month, the engine's best contribution is measuring investable surplus truthfully and steering it, not optimising what is already invested. That makes the Phase-3 leeway and goals work a prerequisite for a useful recommender, not a parallel track. If execution is ever wanted, India's 2026 rules make the broker's own mandates and GTTs, plus a deep link, a safer and cheaper form of "automation" than any order-placing code the app could carry.
